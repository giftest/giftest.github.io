A causal explanation framework for diagnosing and mitigating intersectional bias through subgroup-aware analysis, Shapley-based attribution, and targeted fairness interventions.

THE PROBLEM

Single-attribute fairness can hide harmful intersectional disparities.

A model may appear fair when protected attributes are evaluated independently, while still producing substantial disparities for users who belong to multiple protected groups at the same time. FairAudit addresses this problem by focusing directly on intersectional subgroups and the factors that contribute to unequal outcomes.

FRAMEWORK

A two-stage pipeline for diagnosis and targeted mitigation.

01 Subgroup Discovery Identify intersectional protected-attribute groups.
→
02 Causal Diagnosis Estimate subgroup-aware Shapley contributions to disparity.
→
03 Audit Report Produce interpretable evidence about drivers of unfairness.
→
04 Targeted Mitigation Apply subgroup-specific constraints informed by the diagnosis.

TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Making fairness analysis more actionable and interpretable.

Intersectional Auditing

Evaluated fairness across combinations of protected attributes rather than relying only on single-attribute comparisons.

Causal Attribution

Used causal Shapley-style attribution to identify which feature interactions contribute most strongly to subgroup disparities.

Targeted Mitigation

Derived subgroup-specific mitigation constraints from the diagnostic stage instead of applying the same penalty uniformly across all groups.

Transparent Audit Trail

Structured the workflow so fairness findings can be traced from diagnosis to mitigation and evaluated using explicit subgroup metrics.

EVALUATION

Fairness improvement without sacrificing predictive utility.

FairAudit was evaluated on ACSIncome and CelebA using subgroup disparity metrics, including maximum subgroup disparity, average absolute disparity, disparity reduction, and accuracy-fairness trade-off measures.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Responsible AI needs explanations that lead to concrete engineering decisions.

Fairness metrics can identify that a problem exists, but they do not always explain why it exists or what should change. FairAudit was designed to connect measurement, explanation, and mitigation so that model developers have a more actionable path from detecting unfair behavior to improving the system.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

Trustworthy AI is a systems problem, not just a metric-selection problem.

This project reflects my broader approach to AI engineering: performance, explainability, fairness, and deployment risk should be considered together. Building trustworthy systems requires technical mechanisms that help teams diagnose problems, communicate evidence, and make targeted improvements.
